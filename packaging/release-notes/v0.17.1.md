ZapFast 0.17.1 brings calling: 1:1 voice and video calls, incoming calls that take over the window, and a call history that lives beside your chats. Calling needs PipeWire, and video calls need a camera the kernel exposes under `/dev/video*`.

This is the calling branch, rebased onto upstream `main` at 0.16.5 (`c649917`, the shared-foundation refactor that moved the tray, themes, fonts, and updater into `fastframe`). It no longer sits on the 0.16.1 tree the earlier `v0.17.0` tag was cut from. The release is published from a fork and has **no attached binaries**: the upstream workflow needs the repository secrets and signing keys for its packages, and this fork has neither, so nothing here claims to be a signed or notarized platform build. The change itself is proposed upstream as [crmne/zapfast#220](https://github.com/crmne/zapfast/pull/220).

## New

- **Voice and video calls, one to one.** The phone and camera buttons in a chat's header start a call. An incoming call takes over the window with the caller's name and **Accept** and **Decline**, whoever you were writing to. The duration counts from the moment the two sides are really connected, not from the button press, so a call that rings for a minute and is answered for ten seconds reads as ten seconds.
- **A video call shows both sides.** The peer's picture fills the window, as large as it fits without stretching it, with your own camera in the corner. A rotation the other phone announces is applied, so a phone held upright stays upright here. Turning your camera off stops the video direction and leaves the call and its audio running.
- **Audio device and camera selection, remembered.** The microphone, speaker, and camera pickers list what the machine really has, apply to ZapFast's own call media, and are remembered for the next call. Your system's default devices are never changed. A device that has gone away since it was picked — a headset switched off, a camera unplugged — falls back to the system default, and the call screen says which one it lost.
- **Mute through the call, not around it.** Muting goes through whatsapp-rust's own mute: the outgoing audio stops, the peer is told, and neither the audio stream nor the call is restarted.
- **A call keeps going when you look away.** **Back to the chat** leaves the call running in a bar at the bottom of the window with its length and **Return to the call**. Hiding the window to the tray does not end a call; only hanging up does.
- **Call history.** Finished calls are written to the local archive, listed under **Calls** with their direction, whether they were voice or video, how long they lasted, and how they ended (answered, missed, declined, busy, no answer, connection lost), and shown in the chat they belong to with their length and time.
- **A call that is up but quiet says so.** If the peer's audio stops arriving, a line under the timer names the reason, so a one-sided call is not left looking like a broken microphone on the other end. The log keeps the engine's media counters, so a report about a silent call carries the numbers that explain it. A camera that dies mid-call is reported the same way, without ending the call.

## Limitations

- **Screen sharing is not available in a 1:1 call.** The pinned whatsapp-rust revision carries screen sharing for group calls only, so **Share screen** is shown disabled rather than as a control that cannot work. **OBS Virtual Camera** stands in for it on Linux: start the virtual camera in OBS, then pick it in a call's camera menu.
- **Calls need PipeWire.** ZapFast records through `pw-record` and plays through `pw-play`. Video calls need a V4L2 camera under `/dev/video*`, which includes OBS Virtual Camera.
- **No binaries are attached to this release.** Build from source, or use an upstream release for the packaged application.

## Notes

- The call log is stored in the same encrypted local archive as your messages, is never uploaded, and follows a chat when its privacy-id mapping resolves.
- Nothing about a call is inferred from the button that started it: the state on screen comes from the peer's signaling and the media plane, and a call is only "Connected" once both have said so.
